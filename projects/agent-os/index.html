Problem + Requirements

One of the core problems we set out to solve with AgentOS was “tab overwhelm” the scattered, fractured experience of jumping between tools while trying to synthesize ideas. We wanted to create a space where every part of the creation journey could happen in one place.

Mental Model

The mental model of AgentOS was built around the creation journey: starting with inputs, moving through conversation, and ending with outputs. Users bring in their sources (documents, notes, references), then interact with them through chat by asking questions, clarifying, and synthesizing before transforming those insights into structured outputs like notes, study guides, and Audio Overviews.

By grounding the design in this linear but flexible flow (Inputs → Chat → Outputs) we gave users a clear sense of place within the product while keeping the complexity of new AI interactions digestible and intuitive.

Solution • 3 Panel Structure

It’s rare to find a product that brings reading, writing, and creation together in a truly integrated way largely because juggling all three can be overwhelming. But with AI reducing friction, the opportunity emerged to design a space where every part of the creative process could coexist.

To make that possible, I designed a responsive panel system that adapts to the user’s needs, scaling fluidly while preserving quick access to key elements like sources and notes, even at the smallest sizes.

Panel States

To optimize spatial utility, I created a set of responsive panels that scale based on user needs, retaining essential icons for sources and notes even at minimal widths.

Scalability was a core principle. While the content within these panels can shift and evolve, the underlying system is built to support growth, accommodating new tools, modes, and workflows without breaking the structure.

Key takeaways from the journey

1. Building the right thing > building the thing right

L

Long Geng

PwC Australia

This project reminded me that the true value of design doesn’t come from polishing every pixel or perfecting a screen in isolation. It comes from stepping back and making sure we are solving the right problem in the first place. That shift in mindset set the foundation for everything that followed.

Throughout the journey, I saw how quickly ideas could evolve once we aligned on what really mattered — the user needs, the workflow constraints, and the broader business outcomes that PwC cares about. Whenever we paused to question assumptions or revisit the brief, the product gained clarity, and our decisions became far more intentional.

As the team formed a clearer shared understanding, momentum naturally accelerated. We weren’t just designing faster; we were designing with purpose. That alignment across design, engineering, and product leadership created a sense of confidence that moved the entire project forward in a meaningful way.

What stood out most was how validation prevented us from perfecting solutions that didn’t matter. Early feedback loops — even when they challenged our assumptions — helped us refine direction before refining details. It was a reminder that direction is more important than speed, especially when building something foundational like AgentOS.

In the end, this project reinforced a principle that will guide my work long after this case ends: building the thing right is valuable, but building the right thing is what truly creates impact. When we design with clarity, alignment, and purpose, the craft becomes more than execution — it becomes a way to shape products that actually matter.

2. Here’s what I learned designing a novel, AI-first experience:

L

Long Geng

PwC Australia

UIs will become more dynamic:
AI has unlocked unprecedented possibilities within a single tool. With AgentOS, we aimed to create a seamless experience that supports the entire user journey: Reading → Chat → Creation. Designing for this complexity requires rethinking traditional UI paradigms. The result? A dynamic, adaptive interface that intuitively adjusts to your needs, ensuring fluidity and focus throughout your workflow.

UIs will become more contextually aware:
As AI expands possibilities, the challenge is managing them without overwhelming users. Context-aware interfaces help by proactively suggesting actions to reduce cognitive load. In AgentOS’s case, some users may come to create an audio overview, while others come to chat with their sources. Anticipating these needs and surfacing the right tools/actions at the right moment streamlines workflows and minimizes friction.

We are in a transition period:
Right now, Chat remains the most intuitive and digestible way for users to engage with AI, acting as a familiar anchor that bridges the gap between traditional interfaces and the emerging possibilities of AI-first design. The adaptive UX ensures users can safely rely on chat when they need, while exploring new experiences like the new Audio Overview Interactive Mode.
